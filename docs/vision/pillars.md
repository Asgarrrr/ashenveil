# Pillars

Owned by the human. Ranked: when two pillars conflict, the higher one wins.
They rank first in the authority order: pillars > ADR > design > spec > code.

Status: provisional, approved on 2026-11-17 to start. Revisit them at the
first playable milestone: playing shows whether they settle real disputes.

## 1. Death costs time, never the world: you play a lineage

When your character dies, the world advances until your heir can carry on.
The heir is someone your character bound to them in life: a child, an
apprentice, a disciple. With no heir left, you continue as a stranger.

- The player can never undo a death: no reload, no free resurrection.
- The heir has no protection during the ellipsis. Preparing a succession
  means multiplying its chances: several heirs, guardians, alliances.
- In a shared world, only your lineage waits; the world keeps its pace.

## 2. The simulation never cheats

No scripted event, no invisible nudge, no outcome bent toward the player.
The game chooses what the player learns, never what happens.

- Prefer an honest dull moment over a fabricated exciting one. Answer
  dullness with information: rumors, news, ties to the lineage.
- The level of detail changes precision, never odds: an event resolved far
  from the player has the odds it would have under their eyes.
- World generation is the one place where the game may choose and reject.

## 3. Adventure is the turn; life is the reason

Moment to moment, every turn is a tactical decision: explore, fight, flee,
manage what you carry. Social life, work and family give adventures their
purpose and produce the heirs.

- Prefer a tense turn over a fast one.
- Combat is rare enough to matter and deadly enough to cost a generation.

## 4. Every consequence leaves a trace the character can follow

The character knows their own state in numbers: health, fatigue, skills,
the time cost of an action. The world speaks only through traces:
witnesses, rumors, chronicles, envoys, ruins. A player can always walk a
chain of causes back through the world itself.

- Prefer an in-world trace over a UI readout for anything outside the
  character.
- A system that leaves no readable trace is not finished.

## 5. Depth before breadth

Connect what exists before adding something new. A small world where
everything matters beats a vast world where most things do not.
